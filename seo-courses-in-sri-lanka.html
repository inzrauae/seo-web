How to Evaluate an SEO Course Before You Pay

  1. Check the instructor's own results. Ask which websites they currently rank and for what. An instructor who cannot show live work is teaching from slides.
  2. Look at the syllabus date. Search changes every year. A course that does not mention AI Overviews, generative search, or Core Web Vitals is out of date.
  3. Confirm there is a practical project. You should finish with a website you optimised yourself, not just a certificate.
  4. Ask which tools you will use. Google Search Console and Google Analytics 4 are essential; exposure to a crawler such as Screaming Frog and a keyword tool is a bonus.
  5. Check the language of instruction. Sinhala- or Tamil-medium classes can make technical topics easier, but you will still need English for tools and documentation.
  6. Ask about support after the course. Questions come up when you apply what you learned, often weeks later.
  7. Be wary of ranking guarantees. A course that promises you will “rank #1 in 30 days” is teaching shortcuts that tend to fail.

A 12-Week Self-Study Plan

If you prefer to learn on your own, this plan uses free resources and a small website you control, such as a blog or a friend's business site.

  • Weeks 1–2: Foundations. Read Google's SEO Starter Guide in Search Central and set up Search Console and Google Analytics 4 on your site. Read what SEO is for a plain-language overview.
  • Weeks 3–4: Keyword research. Build a keyword list for one topic, group it by search intent, and map each group to a single page.
  • Weeks 5–6: On-page SEO. Rewrite titles, headings, and meta descriptions for your mapped pages and add internal links between them.
  • Weeks 7–8: Technical SEO. Crawl the site, fix broken links and redirects, check mobile usability, and test speed with PageSpeed Insights.
  • Week 9: Local SEO. Create or audit a Google Business Profile and learn how map rankings work. See the local SEO guide.
  • Week 10: Structured data and GEO. Add schema markup, write clear question-and-answer sections, and learn how AI search tools choose sources. See the AI SEO guide.
  • Week 11: Links and authority. Learn the difference between earned links and link schemes, and pitch one genuine local resource or partner.
  • Week 12: Reporting. Build a monthly report from Search Console showing clicks, impressions, and rankings, and write down what you would change next.

Expect early movement within a few weeks on a low-competition site; competitive terms take months. How long SEO takes explains why.

Questions to Ask Before Enrolling

  • How many hours are hands-on work on a real site, versus lectures?
  • Will I get feedback on my own project, and from whom?
  • When was the course content last updated, and does it cover AI search?
  • Are classes recorded, and for how long can I access them?
  • What do past students do now, and can I speak to one?

Common Questions

SEO Courses in Sri Lanka FAQs

What should I look for in an SEO course in Sri Lanka?

A good course should cover SEO fundamentals, keyword research, on-page and technical implementation, local SEO, and reporting using tools like Google Search Console, plus practical hands-on project work.

Should an SEO course cover GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)?

Yes, modern SEO training should include GEO topics like answer engineering, schema usage, and how AI search platforms like ChatGPT and Gemini surface content, since this is now part of real-world SEO work.

Who benefits most from taking an SEO course?

Business owners looking to reduce paid ad dependency, marketing teams, freelancers, and career changers all benefit from structured SEO training that builds a long-term digital asset instead of one-off campaigns.

Is theory enough, or do I need practical experience too?

Theory alone isn't enough. The best learning combines core concepts with live project feedback, since SEO results depend heavily on applying strategy correctly to a real website.
